# NEAR Ecosystem Mapping for AgriPartners Alpha v1

Research date: 2026-06-19  
Purpose: select the most appropriate first contacts for AgriPartners Alpha v1.  
Scope: strategic research only; this is not a grant application or outreach message.

## How to Read This Document

Statements are labeled as follows:

- **Verified fact:** supported by a current public source listed at the end of this document.
- **Recommendation:** proposed action for AgriPartners.
- **Assumption:** a planning hypothesis that must be tested with the relevant organization.

Fit is assessed against the current AgriPartners position: an agricultural RWA and financing workflow at Alpha v1, using NEAR Testnet for wallet-linked access, smart-contract interaction, withdrawal demonstrations, and verifiable lifecycle signals. It is not an audited, production, or Mainnet deployment.

## Research Summary

### Verified facts

- NEAR's current public positioning centers on open infrastructure for an agent economy, cross-chain liquidity, confidential execution and inference, AI, Intents, and Chain Abstraction.
- NEAR Foundation remains a Swiss non-profit focused on community-driven innovation, ecosystem growth, decentralization, and awareness.
- The official NEAR funding page currently routes builders to Protocol Rewards, DevHub Grants, PotLock, and Meta Pool Grants. It also presents House of Stake governance.
- NEAR DevHub has an active opportunities page on NEARN and an active public GitHub organization with open-source products and repositories.
- The current Horizon landing page is specifically an AI incubation program, even though the NEAR Founder Hub still describes Horizon more broadly as a Web3 accelerator.
- The NEAR Infrastructure Committee accepts proposals and publishes RFPs for critical ecosystem infrastructure.
- No dedicated AgriTech or RWA startup program was identified in the current official NEAR pages reviewed for this brief.

### Strategic conclusion

AgriPartners should enter the ecosystem through a **technical validation and routing conversation**, not through a broad grant request. NEAR DevHub/NEARN is the strongest first entry point because AgriPartners already has implementation evidence and needs technical review, open-source guidance, Testnet-to-Mainnet gap analysis, and routing to current opportunities.

NEAR Foundation is the strongest second contact for ecosystem-level fit and introductions. Protocol Rewards becomes attractive after AgriPartners verifies public development and adoption metrics. Horizon is no longer the default first choice because its active program is AI-focused.

## 1. NEAR Foundation

**Category:** High Fit  
**Priority:** 2

### Mission and current priorities

**Verified fact:** NEAR Foundation describes itself as a Swiss non-profit that enables community-driven innovation, supports ecosystem growth, raises awareness, and champions a decentralized and autonomous ecosystem.

**Verified fact:** Current NEAR public messaging emphasizes the agent economy, AI, Intents, Chain Abstraction, cross-chain liquidity, privacy, and scalable infrastructure. This is broader protocol positioning and should not be interpreted as a published requirement that every supported startup must be AI-native.

### Startup support and grant opportunities

**Verified fact:** NEAR's official Founder Hub directs founders to resources, hackathons, Protocol Rewards, Horizon, and funding information.

**Verified fact:** The current official funding page lists Protocol Rewards, DevHub Grants, PotLock, and Meta Pool Grants rather than promising one universal NEAR Foundation startup grant.

**Recommendation:** Treat NEAR Foundation primarily as an ecosystem steward and routing contact. Ask where a working agriculture-finance product on NEAR should obtain technical review, founder support, RWA/DeFi guidance, and regional ecosystem introductions.

### Why AgriPartners fits

- It demonstrates a concrete non-speculative use of NEAR in a real-world workflow.
- It can broaden the ecosystem narrative beyond AI into agriculture, emerging-market finance, and RWA experimentation.
- It has a working Alpha v1, public repository, pilot narrative, and clear limitations.

Fit is reduced by limited public adoption, Testnet-only status, unaudited contracts, unresolved legal/compliance work, and the absence of a direct match to current AI-centered messaging.

### Recommended contact objective

Obtain an ecosystem-fit assessment and a named referral to the correct technical, founder, incentive, DeFi, or regional pathway. Do not lead with an unrestricted funding request.

## 3. NEAR DevHub / NEARN

**Category:** High Fit  
**Priority:** 1

### Technical ecosystem role

**Verified fact:** NEAR DevHub describes itself through its public GitHub organization as the decentralized home base for NEAR builders.

**Verified fact:** NEARN hosts a current DevHub opportunities page with open, in-review, and completed opportunities and sponsorships. The official NEAR funding page describes DevHub Grants as funding and support for tooling.

### Developer and open-source support

**Verified fact:** The NEAR DevHub GitHub organization was active when checked and contained public repositories including NEARN, Race of Sloths, NEAR Connect, treasury tools, and DevHub contracts.

This makes DevHub the most concrete place in the reviewed map for:

- technical ecosystem feedback;
- open-source contribution discovery;
- builder opportunity discovery;
- tooling and integration guidance;
- referrals to relevant maintainers and funding paths.

### Why AgriPartners fits

- The project can show code, wallet flows, contract interactions, and Testnet evidence.
- It needs a bounded technical review before making Mainnet claims.
- Its immediate questions concern architecture, contract boundaries, wallet UX, verification, and production-readiness gaps.
- The team can potentially establish ecosystem credibility through useful open-source contributions before seeking larger support.

DevHub Grants are described as tooling support. AgriPartners is an application, so direct grant eligibility must not be assumed.

### Recommended contact objective

Request technical fit feedback and routing: identify the top Testnet-to-Mainnet gaps, relevant open-source opportunities, and whether any current DevHub or NEARN pathway fits the project.

### Recommended attached materials

- GitHub repository
- `docs/presentation-readiness/05-near-use-case.md`

- Two- to three-minute demo flow

## 4. NEAR Protocol Rewards

**Category:** High Fit, conditional  
**Priority:** 3

### Mission and current priorities

**Verified fact:** Protocol Rewards describes itself as a transparent on-chain rewards program in which builder activity and impact earn incentives. It states that it offers funding, technical support, feedback, and visibility, with incentives based on progress and impact.

**Verified fact:** NEAR's official funding page describes it as metrics-based and tied to development activity and user adoption.

### Why AgriPartners fits

AgriPartners has a public repository, a functioning Alpha, documented development progress, and a clear product milestone path. These are better suited to a merit-based program than a concept-only application.

Current fit is conditional because the repository's internal readiness scores are not the same as externally verified development or adoption metrics. Real users, transactions, retained usage, releases, contributions, and public product evidence are likely to matter more.

### Recommended contact objective

Confirm eligibility and the exact metrics required for a Testnet Alpha. Determine whether product development, user adoption, open-source contributions, or Mainnet activity drives qualification.

### Recommended attached materials

- GitHub repository
- Release and milestone summary
- Verifiable Testnet activity
- Demo link or curated walkthrough
- Public traction and validation metrics with source notes

## Recommended First Outreach Sequence

### 1. NEAR DevHub / NEARN

Start with a narrow technical and builder-pathway request. This is the most evidence-backed conversation AgriPartners can have today: the team can show code, Testnet flows, architecture, and specific production-readiness questions.

### 2. NEAR Foundation

Use the technical findings from DevHub to make a stronger ecosystem case. Ask for RWA, regional, founder-support, and relevant partner routing rather than generic funding.

### 3. NEAR Protocol Rewards

After verifying public development and usage metrics, ask whether Alpha v1 qualifies and which measurable milestones should be prioritized.

### 4. Proximity Labs

Request specialized feedback on DeFi architecture, settlement, liquidity, Chain Abstraction, and the path from a reporting workflow to a credible financial protocol.

### 5. NEAR Horizon

Request an eligibility decision or referral. Horizon remains useful for startup growth, but the active AI focus makes it a less reliable first entry point for the current product.

This sequence moves from the strongest current evidence to broader strategy: technical validation, ecosystem routing, merit-based incentives, specialized finance feedback, then accelerator eligibility.

## Outreach Failure Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Why it may cause failure | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current NEAR priorities favor AI and Chain Abstraction | Agriculture RWA may look peripheral to current messaging. | Lead with a concrete NEAR workflow and user problem; request routing rather than claiming strategic priority. |
| Testnet-only implementation | Reviewers may see insufficient production evidence. | Show verified Testnet transactions, architecture, limitations, and a realistic Mainnet readiness plan. |
| Weak adoption evidence | Demo profiles and internal readiness scores do not prove traction. | Separate demonstration data from real validation; provide sourced interviews, pilots, users, and usage metrics. |
| Regulatory uncertainty | RWA, investment, lending, custody, and returns can trigger serious legal concerns. | Present a jurisdiction-specific legal work plan and avoid claims of production financial operations. |
| Unaudited contracts | Financial workflows create security expectations. | State the limitation clearly and provide an audit, threat-model, and staged deployment plan. |
| Generic grant-first approach | Broad funding requests are easy to ignore and may reach the wrong team. | Ask for one bounded outcome: technical review, eligibility rule, or named referral. |
| Overstated AI alignment | Adding AI language without a real product need damages trust. | Treat AI ideas as future hypotheses until validated and implemented. |
| Too many attachments | Reviewers may not know where to start. | Send the Launch Kit plus one role-specific artifact and offer the rest. |
| Stale demo assets | Missing or outdated screenshots reduce confidence. | Refresh Marketplace, Portfolio Dashboard, and Funding Progress assets before contact. |
| Inconsistent financial claims | ROI, simple annualized ROI, pilot status, and agreement language may be challenged. | Reconcile every metric across deck, brief, demo, and pilot evidence; label projections and demonstrations. |
| Wrong channel | Core protocol, governance, and infrastructure teams are not startup intake desks. | Follow the ranking and contact each organization only with a mission-matched objective. |
| No clear team profile | Programs assess execution capacity, not only product documents. | Prepare founder roles, technical ownership, agriculture expertise, availability, and 90-day milestones. |

## Final Recommendation

### Recommended first contact

If acting as Head of Ecosystem Partnerships for AgriPartners, I would send the first outreach to **NEAR DevHub through its current NEARN/DevHub builder channel**.

### Why

1. AgriPartners' strongest proof today is technical: a working Alpha, public code, NEAR Testnet wallet flows, and contract interaction.
2. DevHub can evaluate concrete questions without requiring AgriPartners to overstate traction, AI alignment, or grant readiness.
3. A DevHub response can produce a useful result even without funding: architecture feedback, open-source opportunities, technical referrals, and a clearer Mainnet path.
4. Technical validation makes the later NEAR Foundation, Protocol Rewards, and Proximity conversations more credible.
5. Horizon's current AI focus makes it a less precise first destination.

### Next recommended action

Prepare a small technical review packet containing:

- GitHub repository;
- one-page on-chain/off-chain architecture summary;
- `docs/presentation-readiness/05-near-use-case.md`;
- `docs/near-testnet.md`;
- verified Testnet contract and transaction links;
- three specific technical questions;
- a two- to three-minute demo path.

Before sending anything, refresh the missing screenshots and verify every public link. The next contact should ask for technical fit and routing only; it should not be written as a grant application.
