# Investor Protection Framework

## Purpose

The AgriPartners Investor Protection Framework is a model-specific risk management concept intended to reduce an investor's exposure to confirmed agricultural losses. It introduces a Protection Reserve funded over time from a defined portion of the farmer share.

The framework does not remove agricultural, operational, market, counterparty, liquidity, reporting, legal, or technology risk. It is not a promise that invested capital, income, profit, payout, or settlement will be preserved or received.

## Why AgriPartners Needs the Framework

Agricultural investments develop over cycles, while losses may occur before a project has generated enough proceeds to offset the initial investment. A documented protection mechanism can:

- align the farmer and investor around the long-term performance of a deal;
- create a monetary buffer from future farmer proceeds;
- define a transparent process for considering compensation after a Confirmed Loss;
- make residual risk visible rather than presenting an agricultural opportunity as risk-free.

## How the Protection Reserve Works

For a model that adopts the framework:

1. The deal defines a Model-specific reserve rate.
2. After the farmer share is calculated, the specified portion is allocated to the Protection Reserve instead of being immediately available for release.
3. The reserve accumulates gradually as successful cycles produce distributable proceeds.
4. After a Confirmed Loss, all or part of the available reserve may be applied under the deal's approved rules.
5. If no Confirmed Loss requires use of the reserve, the unused balance is intended to be returned to the farmer under the applicable Reserve release rules.

The term Returnable Escrow may be used as a functional description, subject to legal review. It must not imply that a legally regulated escrow arrangement or independent escrow agent already exists.

## Why It Is Funded From the Farmer Share

Funding the reserve from the farmer share makes part of the farmer's future proceeds participate in the risk of the agricultural cycle. It also avoids presenting the reserve as additional investor profit or as money supplied by the platform.

The economic principle is that the reserve is formed from the farmer share and, if there are no Confirmed Losses, the unused balance is intended to be returned to the farmer under established rules. Legal ownership while funds are locked, control of the funds, and creditor treatment remain open legal questions.

## Why the Rate Is Model-Specific

Agricultural models differ in cycle length, expected proceeds, cost structure, loss profile, liquidity, and investor exposure. A rate suitable for one model may be inadequate or excessive for another.

Every model therefore requires its own:

- financial calculation;
- loss and timing scenarios;
- minimum and maximum reserve analysis;
- release schedule;
- legal and operational validation.

Fidlot's 44% rate and Hissar / VariantB's 53% rate are separate model-specific decisions. Neither rate may be copied to another deal or model without an independent calculation.

## Confirmed Loss and Possible Compensation

A Confirmed Loss should be recognized only through an approved process supported by defined evidence. The governing documents must specify who reviews the evidence, who approves the loss, how disputes are handled, and what compensation limit applies.

After approval, the available Protection Reserve may be applied to reduce the investor's confirmed loss. Compensation cannot exceed the available reserve or any lower contractual limit. A reserve that has not yet accumulated cannot be used, so losses in early cycles may leave substantial investor exposure.

No compensation process is final until its product rules, contracts, accounting treatment, and legal authority have been approved.

## Reserve Release

For the current models with a $50,000 initial investment, the following commercial rule is confirmed:

```text
Required reserve =
max($10,000; $50,000 − all cash actually received by the investor)
```

Investor cash includes profit distributions and capital returns. The formula describes the investor's net unrecovered cash position; it does not define legal principal outstanding.

After a confirmed successful cycle, all reserve above the required balance is intended to be released to the farmer. The final $10,000 is intended to be released after project completion and performance of all obligations. Release is suspended following a Confirmed Loss, an overdue mandatory report, default, or an open dispute.

The model-specific amounts appear in the Fidlot and Hissar / VariantB documents. Legal authority, timing, evidence, and appeals must be established in the agreements.

## Why This Is Not a Guarantee

The framework can only use funds actually accumulated and legally available in the reserve. Losses may occur before sufficient funds exist. Evidence may be incomplete or disputed, the farmer or another counterparty may default, and legal or operational restrictions may prevent or delay use of the reserve.

Accordingly, the framework reduces risk under defined conditions; it does not guarantee income, profit, capital preservation, insurance coverage, payout, or settlement.

## Possible Future Platform Module

After financial, product, accounting, technical, and legal validation, the framework could become a configurable platform module. A future module could record a Model-specific reserve rate, reserve contributions, available balances, loss-review status, evidence, approvals, disputes, and Reserve release events.

That future possibility is not current implementation logic. Backend, frontend, database, smart contract, custody, and settlement decisions require separate specifications and approval.
